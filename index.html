Frame 04

The name

الحرمين

It comes from ḥaram — sacred, inviolable, not to be trespassed on. Then the ending -ayn, which in Arabic does something English can’t: it marks exactly two. Al-Ḥaramayn, the two sanctuaries.

Your name is grammatically incapable of being singular. It needs two of something before it means anything at all. I don’t think that’s nothing.

Frame 06

The world, that week

Mariah Carey was at number one in America and had been for most of the summer. And on the day itself, India and Pakistan handed each other’s prisoners back across the border as a gesture of goodwill.

Not a bad week to start in. Someone singing about belonging together, two countries briefly behaving.

Frame 07

Everything above

— the date, the moon, the hours of light, the name and its stubborn dual ending — arrived without your consent. It was all decided before you could weigh in.

The rest of the roll is yours.

In your own words

On being left alone

On what carried you through the year

Harmain,

Everything in the first half of this was handed to you. The date, the moon that wasn’t full, the name that refuses to be singular. You had no say in any of it.

Everything in the second half you answered yourself, and the picture only exists because you did. That felt like the honest way round.

I won’t pretend I know you well. I know enough to be sure you are loved, by more people than you probably count, and I know I’m glad to have your friendship — I don’t hold it lightly.

Happy twenty-one. I hope this year is gentle with you.

— Moiz
